Help

How a scan works

Pick who's watching and press Scan. jellylook reads their recent watch history from Jellystat (or Tautulli on a Plex setup), sends one request to your chosen AI provider for a full batch of suggestions, then adds posters and ratings from TMDb and OMDb. Sorting, filtering and paging never trigger another AI call.

Teaching it what you like

Every card has ♡ Save and ✕ Not for me. Hiding a title removes it and it is never suggested again. Both lists are shown to the AI on the next scan as liked and rejected examples, so the picks shift toward you over time — it takes about three of each before jellylook starts leaning on them.

Feedback belongs to whoever is ticked in the who's watching row, so two people on one server keep separate taste. Tick both and the verdict counts for both. A scan for several people skips anything any of them has hidden.

Automatic scans

Settings → Automatic scans runs a scan on its own every 5 hours, day, week or month. It is off by default. An unattended scan has no one at the keyboard, so it runs for the default user(s) you tick in Settings — pick at least one, or the schedule has nobody to scan for and won't start.

The interval counts from the last scan of any kind, so pressing Scan yourself pushes the next automatic one a full interval out. The clock lives in the database rather than in memory, so a weekly schedule still fires weekly across restarts, and a scan that came due while the container was down runs shortly after it comes back.

It stands aside when it should: never while another scan is running, and not when OMDb's daily lookup quota is nearly spent — a scan you press should always have ratings to work with. A failed automatic scan tries again an hour later rather than waiting out the whole interval. Settings → Status shows the schedule, when the next one is due, and whether the last one failed.

Turn this on together with Auto-send to Seerr and jellylook runs unattended end to end: it finds new titles on your schedule and requests the ones above your match %, with no one watching. Scans that ran by themselves are marked automatic in the header and in the From dropdown, so a batch of overnight requests can always be traced back to the scan that filed them.

Auto-sending high matches to Seerr

Settings → Auto-send to Seerr sets a match % at or above which jellylook files the Seerr request for you. It is off until you choose a threshold, and it runs at the end of every scan — one you started, or one the schedule above ran for you.

Titles already in your library, already requested, or already available in Seerr are skipped. TV shows follow your TV request mode; on Ask each time — which has nobody to ask during a scan — the whole show is requested. Anything sent this way carries an ⤒ Auto-sent badge on its poster and an Auto-sent to Seerr ✓ button, and the status line names what went out. If Seerr is unreachable the scan still finishes and nothing is requested.

Latest scan vs All time

The From dropdown switches between the newest batch, a specific earlier scan, and All time — every title suggested inside the retention window, newest sighting first, with duplicates and hidden titles removed.

What the match % means

It's the AI's own estimate of how close a title sits to your recent viewing, from 0 to 100. Directionally useful, not science.

"Because you watched"

Each suggestion names the specific title in your history that inspired it, so you can see the connection at a glance.

Where to watch

Streaming availability is shown for the country set by TMDB_REGION, and is stored when a title is scanned — change the region and new scans pick it up. Data is provided by JustWatch via TMDb.

Housekeeping

Results are kept for RETENTION_DAYS (60 by default). Titles you save are exempt from that, but nothing survives past MAX_RETENTION_DAYS — one year — so the database cannot grow forever. Your save/hide decisions themselves are never deleted, so a hidden title stays hidden even after its card has aged out.

The .env knobs

LLM_PROVIDER switches between anthropic, openai, google, openwebui and ollama (with LLM_MODEL for the model name). On anthropic, LLM_FALLBACK_MODEL is tried when the first model fails and LLM_EFFORT sets how hard it thinks. HISTORY_SOURCE switches between jellystat, tautulli (Plex) and jellyfin. RECS_PER_SCAN, PER_PAGE and RETENTION_DAYS control batch size, paging and how long results are kept. TMDB_REGION sets the streaming country. Restart the container after changing .env.

Troubleshooting

Source unreachable — a scan that fails immediately usually means the history source (Jellystat, Tautulli or Jellyfin) can't be reached or the API key is wrong. Check the container logs.
Seerr down — cards still render; the Add button is disabled until Seerr answers again.
OMDb limit — the free key allows 1000 lookups a day. The cache keeps re-scans nearly free; today's count shows in Settings.
